About Me

Most software is built on top of other software, and most of that was written by someone who was tired, on a deadline, and reasonably sure nobody would ever read it again. This site is about the layers underneath — the parts that have to keep working whether or not anyone is paying attention.

I spend my time on backend services, systems programming, and the infrastructure that holds them together. That means a lot of terminals, a lot of logs, and a slow-growing appreciation for anything that fails loudly instead of quietly. I've written things in Python, Java, C, Go, and JavaScript, and I've broken things in all of them.

The projects here range from deliberate learning exercises to things I built because an existing tool annoyed me. A few are polished. Most are honest. If you're looking for a particular one, the projects page has them sorted roughly by how much I'd still defend the design decisions.

Everything on this site was written and assembled by hand. If something looks off, it probably is, and I'd rather hear about it than not.
